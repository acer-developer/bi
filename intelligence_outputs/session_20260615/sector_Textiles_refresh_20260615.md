## Why This Sector, Why Now

The Indian Textiles sector is in a **structural rater crisis**. With 63.7% of rated companies marked INC, this is not random default — it represents a systemic breakdown between Textiles SMEs and incumbent rating agencies.

**Root causes of high Textiles INC rate:**
1. Many Textiles SMEs are in clusters (Surat, Tirupur, Bhilwara, Ludhiana) — when one agency loses trust in a cluster, it marks all of them INC en masse
2. Seasonal cash flow volatility in Textiles makes it hard to submit consistent financials
3. Mid-tier agencies (Brickwork, Acuité, Infomerics) took on large Textiles books during the MSME rating push — then abandoned them when resources tightened

**ACER opportunity:** The 214 INC Textiles companies are not bad businesses. They are **disconnected businesses** — they had a rating, lost the relationship, and now have open credit facilities without support. ACER can step in as the rescuer.

**Timing note:** Post-monsoon (October–November) is typically when Textiles companies resume capital expenditure. June–August is the right time to approach and begin the onboarding process so ratings are ready before the buying season.

## Instrument Mix

Textiles sector instrument mix is dominated by working capital:
- **Fund-based limits** (CC, OD, packing credit): ~60% of Textiles instruments
- **Term loans** (capex for looms, processing machines): ~25%
- **Non-fund based** (BG, LC for import of raw materials): ~15%

**Key implication:** Textile companies renew working capital limits annually with their bank. If their rating has lapsed (INC), the bank cannot renew the CC/OD limit. This is an **active credit emergency** — not a future problem. The sales call should frame it as: *"Your bank cannot renew your CC limit without a valid rating."*
